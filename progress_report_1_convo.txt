4. Challenges / Problems
Write at least 1 specific challenge (or more!) that you are currently facing, such as:

        •       Something you don’t understand yet
        •       A bug you can’t fix
Note: Be specific. Don’t just type “this feature is hard because I don’t know how to do it.”

One challenge I am actively working through is keeping a consistent look and feel while the project is still changing quickly.
Because I want the UI to stay generic and reusable, I rely on shared theme tokens, reusable layout components, and common section patterns instead of page-specific styling.
The difficult part is that when one theme variable or reusable component changes, it can affect multiple areas at once (for example spacing, contrast, or visual hierarchy).
I am addressing this iteratively by making smaller UI updates, validating each change in the Home and Mentors flow, and then stabilizing before introducing new design changes.
This approach is helping me move toward a cleaner, more stable interface without losing consistency.

const visibleSections = getPrimarySiteSectionsForRequest();

<LinkCardGrid

I picked this Home page section because it was one of the hardest parts for me to design in a clean, reusable way.
What makes it interesting is that one source of truth (visibleSections) controls both what users see on the screen and what search engines read through JSON-LD.
That matters to me because I did not want the app to "say" one thing to students and a different thing to search engines.
I also put real effort into keeping Header and Footer global through layout components, because they make the app feel stable: the Header gives students predictable navigation and trust cues, and the Footer keeps safety messaging and important links visible from every page.
Personally, this section taught me how strong architecture choices can reduce future bugs, since adding or updating sections in one place now automatically flows into navigation, metadata, and page structure.
